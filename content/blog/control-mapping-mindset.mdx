Most compliance training walks you through one standard at a time: here is ISO 27001, here is
SOC 2, here is NIST CSF. You memorize each document in isolation — and then panic when an auditor
asks how they relate.

## The problem with document-first learning

Standards overlap enormously. "Access control" appears in ISO 27001 Annex A, NIST CSF's Protect
function, SOC 2's Common Criteria, GDPR Article 32, and CIS Control 5/6 — and it is implemented by
the *same* technical primitives: IAM policies, RBAC, MFA.

Learning each standard separately hides that shared backbone. You end up re-learning the same control
five times under five names.

## Control-first learning

Flip it around. Learn the **control** once — what it is, why it matters, how to implement it in the
cloud — then see every framework that requires it.

<Callout title="Try it">
Open the [Control Explorer](/control-mapping) and filter by a framework. Notice how the same handful
of controls satisfy requirements across all of them.
</Callout>

That is the whole idea behind this academy: **controls are the unit of learning, frameworks are the
mappings.** It is faster, it sticks better, and it matches how real security work is done.
